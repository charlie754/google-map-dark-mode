Google Map Dark Mode

Gives Google Maps the dark cartography Google already draws for its own night mode, and darkens the panels and controls around it to match. Nothing is inverted and nothing is filtered.

Dark mode

The master switch. Off leaves Google Maps exactly as it comes: the redirect rules are switched off in the browser, not merely ignored, so nothing of this extension is in the way of a page load.

How this works

Google draws its base map from a palette file it publishes itself, in a light version and a dark one. With dark mode on, the extension redirects the browser’s request for the light palette to Google’s own dark file. That is the whole trick for the map: what you see is Google’s real night cartography, drawn by Google’s renderer, not an inversion or a filter laid over the top. It is also why the map does not go muddy or turn photographs into negatives.

The panels around the map are a different problem. Maps colours them from a few hundred CSS custom properties whose names are machine-generated and change on every Google release, so a theme written against names or class selectors would break within weeks. Instead the extension reads the properties out of the page as they are, converts each colour to a darker one in OKLCH — a colour space where changing the lightness leaves the hue where it was — and writes them back. It never touches the map canvas, and it leaves photographs, avatars and shadows alone.

There is no account, no server of the extension’s own, and no analytics. The only requests it makes are to google.com and gstatic.com, the same servers Maps is already talking to, and only to check that the dark palette is still being served.

Known limitations

  • Satellite and Street View are not darkened. They are photographs. Darkening a photograph does not produce a night view, it produces a broken one, so the extension leaves them exactly as they are.
  • The Google account bar in the top right stays light. That strip is drawn outside the colour system the rest of Maps uses, with its colours written into the page directly, so there is nothing there to remap. The Sign-in button in particular stays bright against the dark interface.
  • Terrain is untested. There are rules covering the terrain base map as well as the ordinary road one, but the terrain view has never been driven through a real session on either browser. Treat it as unverified rather than supported: it may look right, it may not, and it may behave differently in Chrome and Firefox.
  • Not every Google address is covered. Maps is served from dozens of country domains, and this extension carries a fixed list of the most used ones. On an address outside that list it does nothing at all, and no setting here will change that. Covered right now: .
  • This depends on Google internals that Google does not document and has never promised to keep. A change on their side can stop it working overnight, and no setting here would fix it — a new version of the extension would be needed. That is what the status section above is for: it is the only way to tell the difference between “Google changed something” and “I turned it off and forgot”.
